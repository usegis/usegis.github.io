Address data

A precinct lookup needs to know which house numbers are in which precinct. Whatever a county publishes is turned into street ranges: for each street in a zipcode, runs of house numbers and their precinct. The better the addresses, the better the ranges, best first.

1 Street data

Each address with its precinct, from the elections office's own system, after each redraw.

How it's used: grouped into street ranges, each keeping the county's precinct.

Result: the county's own answer for every address.

Elections

2 Address points

Each address at its location: house number, street and zipcode.

How it's used: each point placed on the precinct map, then grouped into street ranges.

Result: right wherever the precinct map is current.

GIS or addressing

3 Parcels

Parcel shapes with each parcel's site address and parcel number.

How it's used: each parcel's center stands in for an address point. Only the site address and parcel number are read, never owner names or mailing addresses.

Result: nearly as good as address points, missing homes with no site address on file.

Assessor or GIS

4 Census ranges

Census TIGER's ranges of possible house numbers on each side of each block.

How it's used: each side placed on the precinct map.

Result: fine in towns, but often missing or wrong for rural addresses.

Census, when a county publishes no addresses

The result

The street ranges and precinct outlines are small files, one for each zipcode. The California Precinct Lookup reads them in the browser, and the precinct-lookup library does the same in any app. A typed address never leaves the browser, and no voter data is used.
