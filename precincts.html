Precinct maps

A precinct map is a set of shapes, one for each precinct, each with its precinct number. Counties keep it as a shapefile or GIS layer and redraw it before elections. A lookup can get it four ways, best first.

1 Live layer

The precinct map on the county's GIS server, such as ArcGIS, updated after each redraw. A lookup always reads the current lines, and no one has to send a file.

Elections, published by GIS

2 Download

A dated file, a shapefile or GeoJSON, on the county's website or GIS hub. Current until the next redraw, when someone has to post the new one.

Elections or GIS

3 On request

Emailed by the elections office, free under Elections Code §12263. Current when sent, but every requester has to ask, after every redraw.

Elections

4 Statewide Database

The Statewide Database (SWDB) publishes every county's precinct map as GeoJSON, months after each election. Useful by itself, but any redraw since then is missing.

SWDB, from what counties send it

What makes a precinct map useful

Precinct numbers as the voter file writes them (registration precincts, not only consolidated voting precincts), and the date or election the lines are for.
